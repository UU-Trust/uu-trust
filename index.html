Uniting Users (UU)

Human-Origin Infrastructure to Preserve Human Agency

Human-Origin Infrastructure for the People.

The internet was built for people. As artificial intelligence, automation, bots and synthetic identities become increasingly capable of acting like people, the internet faces a fundamental challenge: how can we distinguish human-origin participation from machine-generated activity without asking people to surrender their identity, personal data or privacy?

Uniting Users (UU) is a public-interest initiative working to build Human-Origin Infrastructure: a neutral, permissionless infrastructure layer designed to help digital systems distinguish human-origin participation on the internet.

Our ambition is to bring a new layer of trust to the digital world while preserving human agency, privacy and freedom. UU is designed to complement the platforms, communities and digital ecosystems people already use—not replace them.

A more trustworthy internet should be possible without giving up the principles that made the internet valuable in the first place.

What do we mean by Human-Origin Infrastructure?

A new, privacy-preserving, permissionless and neutral infrastructure layer intended to help systems distinguish human-origin participation without centralized identity custody, biometric databases or surveillance architecture.

Why bring UU into the public when it is not yet built?

The objective is to create infrastructure that can support a more trustworthy digital environment for people while preserving the freedom, privacy and neutrality that the internet was built upon.

UU is being developed as a complementary and neutral layer for the internet that can coexist with platforms and existing digital ecosystems. Its long-term purpose is to serve the public, rather than be owned or controlled by a single company.

Why Human-Origin Infrastructure?

The digital world is changing rapidly. Artificial intelligence can generate content, operate software, interact with people and participate in digital environments at an unprecedented scale.

As these capabilities grow, distinguishing human-origin participation from automated or synthetic activity becomes increasingly important. This is not simply a question of identifying bots. It is a broader question about trust, human agency and the future relationship between people and the digital systems around them.

How can digital environments recognize human-origin participation while respecting individual privacy?

How can we build trust without creating more centralized identity systems, surveillance mechanisms or databases of personal information?

And how can this infrastructure serve people across different platforms, communities and countries, rather than being controlled by a single ecosystem?

These are the questions that motivate Uniting Users.

A new layer for the internet—not another platform

UU is being developed as a complementary infrastructure layer that can coexist with existing platforms and digital ecosystems. The ambition is not to replace the services people use, but to explore a shared foundation that can help distinguish human-origin participation across the wider internet.

The principles guiding this mission are:

  • Human agency: Technology should serve people, not make authentic human participation increasingly difficult to distinguish.
  • Privacy: Distinguishing human-origin participation should not require people to surrender their personal information.
  • Neutrality: Infrastructure intended for the public should not depend on the interests of one platform or a single company.
  • Permissionless participation: The infrastructure is intended to be open to participation across existing digital environments.
  • Public interest: The long-term objective is to build infrastructure for people and the wider digital ecosystem, rather than an asset designed solely around private ownership.

These are the principles guiding the development of UU. The protocol is still in its pre-deployment phase, and the work required to turn this vision into functioning, globally deployable infrastructure remains ahead.

Inviting People to Build UU Together

Infrastructure of this potential importance should not be a conversation reserved for engineers, technology companies or investors. It is a question that concerns everyone who uses the internet.

The UU Mission begins with a simple idea: infrastructure intended to help distinguish human-origin participation should be built with people and for people.

You do not need to be a technical expert to participate in this journey. You can help by learning about the mission, asking questions, sharing the idea with others, following the development of UU, contributing your expertise or connections, or supporting the work financially if you are in a position to do so.

Every person who helps others understand why Human-Origin Infrastructure may matter can contribute to building awareness around the mission.

Understanding before participation. Transparency before commitment. Building with people, for people.

The First UU OGs and One Million Founding Supporters

The first 10,000 people who stand behind the mission are intended to become the First UU OGs—the earliest supporters who believed that this infrastructure was worth helping to build.

The longer-term ambition is to bring together one million Founding Supporters.

The idea behind this goal is straightforward: one million people each making a small contribution—one coffee invitation—could help move the project toward making this infrastructure available to the public.

The goal is not simply to raise money. It is to build a community of people who understand why this work matters and want to help make it possible.

Every Founding Supporter will be part of the public story of how UU came into existence and how the mission developed.

If the mission resonates with you, you can help build awareness, join the community or contribute to the work.

Explore the Work Behind UU

For readers who want to explore the history, earlier research and public protocol materials behind the mission.

Uniting Users is the outcome of an extensive period of research, development, experimentation and architectural exploration.

Between 2018 and 2025, prior research and development took place under LinksUU Technology Solutions Ltd in the UK. This seven-year period involved multiple projects, approaches and architectural pivots, providing the research foundation from which the current UU architecture emerged.

The prior work remains part of the history of how UU came to exist. UU Protocol formally launched as a protocol direction in 2026, and the current public specification is in its pre-deployment phase. The published materials are available for transparency, research and technical discussion.

These materials provide additional context for readers who wish to go deeper. They are available alongside the public-facing mission so that people can choose the level of detail they want to explore.
